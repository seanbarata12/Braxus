Sewer & drain line replacement

When a drain keeps backing up after it's been cleared, the pipe itself is usually the problem: cracked, collapsed, sagging or full of roots. We replace broken drain pipe under the basement floor and outside, from the house to the City property line.

What we replace

  • Sanitary drain under the basement floor, including branches
  • Exterior sewer line from the house to the City property line
  • Basement floor drains
  • Cast iron and clay pipe that has cracked or collapsed
  • Weeping tile connections to the sanitary drain, when they need to come off

Signs of a broken drain

  • Backups that come back weeks after the drain was cleared
  • Sewer smell in the basement
  • Slow drains throughout the house at the same time
  • Water or sewage coming up through the floor drain
  • Roots found when the line was snaked

How it works

  1. 1Camera inspectionWe run a camera through the line to find exactly where and how bad the damage is.
  2. 2QuoteYou get a written quote with the length of pipe and the work involved.
  3. 3ReplaceWe excavate, replace the pipe and set it on proper bedding so it doesn't sag again.
  4. 4RestoreBackfill and compact, then new concrete inside or rough grade outside.

What affects the price

  • How much pipe needs replacing, and how deep it is
  • Inside under the slab versus outside under the lawn or driveway
  • Floor finishes, walls or landscaping in the way
  • Adding a backwater valve while the line is open, which can qualify for the Toronto rebate

Drain & sewer line repair FAQ

Something else? Call or text (647) 468-9696.

How do I know if my sewer line is broken?

Repeated backups after cleaning, sewer smells and slow drains everywhere are the usual signs. A camera inspection confirms it, and shows us where the break is so we only dig where we need to.

How much does it cost to replace a sewer line in Toronto?

It depends on the length and depth of the pipe and whether it's inside or outside. We give a free written quote after the camera inspection.

Should I add a backwater valve while the drain is open?

Usually yes. The floor is already open, so it costs less to add one then, and in Toronto the valve qualifies for a subsidy of up to $1,600.

Is the City responsible for my sewer line?

The City is responsible for the sewer from the main to the property line. From the property line into the house is the homeowner's responsibility. If roots or damage are on the City side, we'll tell you so you can call them.
